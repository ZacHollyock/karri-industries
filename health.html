The Three Pillars

01

Mobility

Keeping joints healthy and moving through a full range of motion.

  • Static stretches held for 30 seconds
  • 2–4 sets per muscle group
  • 5 days a week, around 5 minutes per muscle group each week
  • Stretch gently, at about 30–40% of discomfort, not to the point of pain
  • Stretch when warm, after a session or a walk
02

Aerobic

Building the heart, lungs and endurance base everything else sits on.

  • Zone 2: a pace where I can still hold a conversation, breathing through my nose where possible
  • Moderate: around 35 minutes at a pace where talking is hard
  • HIIT: short, all-out efforts with short rests, repeated in rounds
  • Any format works: running, cycling, rowing, swimming, hiking
03

Resistance

Building and keeping strength and muscle for the long term.

  • Schedule A (strength): 4–8 reps, 3–4 sets, 2–4 min rest
  • Schedule B (hypertrophy): 8–15 reps, 2–3 sets, about 90 sec rest
  • Alternate between A and B every month
  • Two exercises per muscle group: one loading the shortened position, one the lengthened position
  • 50–60 minutes of work after a warm-up, 75 minutes max

Principles I Follow

  1. Consistency beats perfection. If I miss a day, I shift it by a day or double up. The week matters more than any single session.
  2. Progressive overload. Slowly add weight, reps or time so the body keeps adapting.
  3. Warm up first. Around 10 minutes before lifting, plus hip or shoulder mobility for that day's muscles.
  4. Breathe to recover. Use a physiological sigh between sets (two inhales through the nose, one long exhale) and 3–5 minutes of slow breathing after training.
  5. Time the cold. No ice bath straight after weights. Wait 6–8 hours, or do it before training.
  6. Listen to recovery. After poor sleep or when sick, train lighter or rest.

Nutrition

Principles from Peter Attia's Outlive and Andrew Huberman's Protocols

Attia

Energy Balance

How much I eat matters more than which diet I follow.

  • Eat for my goal: maintain, build or lean out
  • Use Attia's three levers: eat less, cut out certain foods, or eat within a shorter window
  • Always pull at least one lever, never none
Attia & Huberman

Protein First

The nutrient I'm least flexible on. It protects muscle for the long term.

  • At least 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day
  • Spread across the day in meals of 40–50 g
  • Choose foods with a high protein-to-calorie ratio: eggs, lean meat, fish
Huberman

Food Quality

Mostly whole foods, built around protein and plants.

  • Mostly minimally processed foods
  • Plenty of vegetables and fruit for fibre and micronutrients
  • Limit ultra-processed foods and added sugar
Huberman

Gut Health

A diverse gut microbiome supports immunity and lower inflammation.

  • Eat low-sugar fermented foods daily: yoghurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi
  • Buy them from the fridge section so they have live cultures
  • Start with 1 serving a day and build up slowly

The Shopping List

Foods with the strongest evidence for long-term health

🥦

Vegetables

  • Leafy greens: spinach, kale, lettuce, rocket
  • Cruciferous: broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, Brussels sprouts
  • Colourful: carrots, capsicum, sweet potato, green beans

Linked to lower heart disease, stroke and cancer risk. Benefits keep rising up to about 800 g of fruit and veg a day.

🫐

Fruit

  • Berries: blueberries, strawberries, raspberries
  • Apples & pears
  • Citrus: oranges, mandarins, lemons

Apples, pears, citrus and berries are among the fruits most strongly linked to lower early death.

🐟

Protein

  • Oily fish: salmon, sardines, mackerel
  • Eggs
  • Lean meat: chicken, turkey, lean beef
  • Greek yoghurt and cottage cheese

Fish is linked to lower mortality. Eggs and lean meats give the most complete protein per calorie.

🌰

Nuts, Seeds & Legumes

  • Nuts: walnuts, almonds, hazelnuts (a handful a day)
  • Seeds: chia, flax, pumpkin
  • Legumes: lentils, chickpeas, black beans

Nuts show one of the strongest links to lower mortality of any food. Legumes add fibre and plant protein.

🌾

Whole Grains

  • Rolled oats
  • Brown rice and quinoa
  • Wholegrain or sourdough bread

Low whole grain intake is one of the leading dietary risks for early death worldwide.

🫒

Fats & Fermented Foods

  • Extra virgin olive oil as my main oil
  • Avocado
  • Fermented: plain yoghurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi

Olive oil cut heart attack and stroke risk by about 30% in the PREDIMED trial. Fermented foods support gut health.

🚫

Limit

  • Processed meat: bacon, ham, salami, sausages
  • Red meat: keep to moderate amounts
  • Sugary drinks and added sugar
  • Ultra-processed and very salty foods

The WHO classes processed meat as a known cause of bowel cancer. Sugary drinks, processed meat and salt are among the biggest dietary risks globally.
